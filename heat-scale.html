How to read it

What the numbers actually mean

The index is ours, not an industry standard. It ranks our sauces against each other, which is the comparison that matters when you are picking a bottle off the shelf.

1–2 · Mild

Flavor is doing the work. You can pour these freely and nobody at the table will complain.

3–6 · Medium to medium-hot

Real heat that shows up and then moves on. This is where most people find their everyday bottle.

7–10 · Hot and up

Habanero and above. Start with a drop, wait a minute, then decide whether you want a second.

Pepper reference

The Scoville numbers behind the peppers

These are published ranges for the raw peppers, not measurements of our finished sauces — a sauce is always milder than the pepper it is built on, because everything else in the bottle dilutes it.

Poblano
1,000 – 2,000 SHU — body and green flavor, almost no burn
Jalapeño
2,500 – 8,000 SHU — the reference point most people know
Serrano
10,000 – 23,000 SHU — brighter and sharper than jalapeño
Habanero
100,000 – 350,000 SHU — fruity up front, serious heat behind it
Trinidad Scorpion
1,200,000 – 2,000,000 SHU — among the hottest peppers grown

Pepper heat varies with the growing season, so treat every number here as a range rather than a promise.

If you overdo it

Water will not save you

Capsaicin is oil-soluble, so water just moves it around. Whole milk, yogurt, sour cream and ice cream all work because the fat actually carries it away.

Bread, rice and a spoonful of sugar help too. Give it four or five minutes — the peak passes faster than it feels like it will.

Reach for dairy

Milk, yogurt or sour cream. Fat binds the capsaicin, water does not.

Add starch

Bread or rice gives the oil something to hold on to.

Wait it out

Most of the burn is gone inside five minutes. Do not chase it with more water.
